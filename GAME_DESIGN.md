# Park Laps: Game Design

A small, physics-driven 3D freeski game that runs in Chrome on school Chromebooks. The feel is modeled on Shredsauce: momentum, crouch-to-pop, and rotations built from separate spin and flip axes, so corks come from combining them. The point is riding and landing clean; an optional combo score (see Scoring) rewards tricks chained together, never a whole run.

## Core feel
- Speed comes from the slope and from pole pushes. Crouching lowers drag.
- Landing angle matters. Skis must roughly match the slope and the body must be upright. Land clean and you ride away; land badly and you bail.
- Simple but not arcade: no auto-rotation and no auto-landing. A small landing tolerance (set in config) keeps it learnable.
- Skis only. Riding switch (backwards) is supported, and the camera follows the direction of travel.
- Spin speed at takeoff inherits from how hard you're carving into the lip, plus any held spin input.

## Controls (default keys, all rebindable in Settings)
The right hand works the arrow keys. The left hand handles grabs, pole push, and checkpoints.

| Key | On the ground | In the air | On a rail |
|---|---|---|---|
| ← / → | Turn | Spin left / right | Rotate skis (free, any angle) |
| ↑ hold, then release | Crouch (less drag), then pop | Front flip | Crouch, then pop off |
| ↓ | Brake | Back flip | Brake (drags the skis on the rail) |
| Shift | Pole push | — | — |
| Q / E | — | Lincoln loop left / right | — |
| Tab | Set personal checkpoint | — | — |
| R | Respawn at last checkpoint (hold 1s: back to top) | same | same |
| M | Trail map (the game waits; M or Esc closes it) | same | same |
| Esc or P | Pause menu (P also works in fullscreen, where the browser takes Esc) | same | same |

Grabs (hold in the air, release to let go):

| Key | Grab | Key | Grab |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Left mute | Z | Right mute |
| S | Left safety | X | Right safety |
| D | Left japan | C | Right japan |
| F | Left tail | V | Right tail |
| G | Left nose | B | Right nose |

Style moves (hold in the air, release to let go; one of each, no left and right):

| Key | Move | Key | Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| T | Screamin' seaman: a daffy with the legs crossed, skis still pointing ahead | 1 | Iron cross: heels up behind, skis crossed flat in an X behind the legs |
| W | Daffy: one ski forward, one back | 2 | Spread eagle: arms and legs flung out wide |

- **Butters:** on the snow, a nose grab key (G/B) presses onto the ski tips and a tail grab key (F/V) onto the tails. While pressed, ←/→ pivot the skis on that end (a butter 180 or 360) instead of carving, and you keep sliding the way you were going. Releasing ↑ pops off that end (an ollie off the tails, a nollie off the tips), a bit stronger than a flat pop, and the pivot carries on as spin. Landing with a nose or tail grab still held goes straight into the press.
- Holding a spin key and a flip key together tilts the rotation axis. That produces corks and misty flips.
- Rotation continues while a key is held and slows after release. Letting go early is how you time the landing.
- Grabs are poses: the hand reaches for that part of the ski. A grab can be held through rotations. Style moves are poses too, with no hand on a ski. Holding a newer grab or move key switches to it; letting go falls back to another one still held. Grabs and style moves don't change the physics.
- Test multi-key combos (two arrows + grab + Shift) on a real Chromebook keyboard. If a combo drops keys, change the default binding.

## Crashes
- **Bail triggers:** landing with the skis too far off the slope angle, landing on your back or head, or hitting an obstacle hard.
- **On a bail:** the skier tumbles and slides to a stop. This is a cheap procedural tumble driven by the sim, not a full ragdoll. After about 1.5s the screen fades and you respawn at your most recent checkpoint. Pressing R skips the wait.
- **Crash setting** (Settings, Crashes): *Respawn by itself* (the default, as above) or *Wait for respawn key*: the skier stays down in the snow until you press R.
- A full ragdoll using Rapier is a possible later upgrade.

## Rails and boxes
- **Physics feel with a light magnet.** If the skis come down onto a rail or box within a small snap distance (set in config), the skier locks onto the rail's line and slides along it, with friction slowing them.
- There is no grind button. You get on by landing on the rail.
- You land on with the skis roughly straight (parallel) or sideways (perpendicular), and that angle carries over. On the rail ←/→ rotate the skis freely; they stay at whatever angle you leave them.
- You leave by riding off the end or by popping off with ↑. Spinning off is allowed.
- Holding ↓ brakes: the skis drag against the rail or box (`rails.brakeDecel`, 6 m/s², ramping in like the snow brake), enough to stop on any rail in the maps, even the long rails down 19° stretches. On a metal rail the sparks come thicker. The skis stay at their angle (no hockey-stop swing); the body sinks a little.
- Too far off-angle or off-center and you slip off. That's not a bail unless you then land badly.
- v1 features: flat box, flat rail, down rail, flat-down rail. Rails are defined as polylines in the map JSON.

## Checkpoints and runs
- Each map has flags. Riding past a flag saves it.
- **Tab** sets a personal checkpoint at your current position, heading, and speed, so you can re-hit a jump with the same approach. It works only while on the ground.
- Respawning (R, or after a bail) goes to whichever checkpoint was set most recently, personal or flag.
- Holding R for 1s sends you back to the top.
- Every map has a finish zone at the bottom. Entering it shows an on-screen **Back to top** button (Enter also works). Back to top is also in the pause menu.

## Scoring (milestone 6g)
Asked for after 6f: a score made of flow, combos and stacking multipliers. It's time based, so it goes away when you stop hitting things, and it never scores a whole run. It can be switched off in Settings and never runs on the Slalom. Every number is in `config.ts`'s `score` block.

- **Combos.** Each trick adds points and multiplier to the live combo and tops its timer up to 4 s. The timer drains on the snow. In the air it drains at half speed and can't run out (it holds at 0.75 s); on a rail it holds. When it runs out on the snow the combo is **banked**: its points times its multiplier. A crash **loses** it, so nothing banks. Respawning (R) or going back to the top banks a live combo if you're riding the snow, and loses it if you're in the air or on a rail. The timer runs on game time: Slow motion slows it, and the pause menu and trail map stop it.
- **What scores:**
  - Spins: 150 per 180°, counted when you land within 45° of it. Corks (spinning with 50° or more off-axis) earn 50% more. Spins onto or off a rail count in 90s from 180 ("270 on", "180 off").
  - Flips and Lincoln loops: 400 per full rotation, counted within 90°. A flip with a spin is a rodeo (back) or a misty (front) with its spin ("Rodeo 540"). Doubles and triples are named.
  - Grabs: any grab held 0.2 s or more scores 100, plus 100 per second held (up to 2 s). Grabs score generically from the grab list (`GRAB_ACTIONS`), so new grabs score with no change. A trick names its longest-held grabs, up to three ("Cork 720 · Left mute").
  - Airtime: 50 per second on any trick or jump.
  - Rails and boxes: 30 per meter slid (from 1 m, up to 40 m), ×1.5 sideways, ×1.25 switch. Past 40 m a slide keeps the combo alive (the timer holds on a rail) but scores no more, so the Park's top-to-bottom rail (1,948 m) scores like a long rail rather than outscoring every trick. Named by what you slid and how: "Rail 50-50 13 m", "Box slide", "Log slide", "Switch ledge 50-50".
  - Butters (added after the butters arrived): a nose or tail press pivoted on the snow scores 75 per 180° turned (counted like a spin: "Tail butter 180", "Nose butter 360"), plus 50 per second pressed (up to 3 s). A press held 0.6 s or more with no pivot is a "Nose press" or "Tail press". Each is a trick: it tops the timer up and adds multiplier, with the usual repeat penalty, so butters link features across the flats. Popping out of a press is an ollie (tails) or a nollie (tips): the air's trick takes the name ("Ollie 180", "Nollie 360 · Left mute") and adds 25%.
  - A big air with no trick (1.2 s or more, longer than a pop on the flat) scores as "Air" and tops the timer up, but adds no multiplier.
  - **Flow:** carving hard at speed (edge angle and speed) slows the drain by up to 60% and adds up to 25 points a second. A small air with no trick adds 40 per second of air. Flow never starts a combo or tops one up, so cruising with no tricks still ends it.
- **Landings.** A clean landing adds 25%. A sketchy one (past 75% of the crash limit nose up or down, 50% of the edge or sideways limits, or 90% of the hardest impact) scores half and adds no multiplier. Taking off switch and landing switch each add 25% ("Switch" before the name, "Switch landing" after it). With No crashes on, a landing the cheat saves counts as sketchy.
- **Multiplier.** Each different trick in a combo adds 1, so a combo of one trick is ×1. A trick is its name plus its grabs, so changing the grab makes it a different trick. A repeat scores half what the time before scored and adds half as much multiplier (nothing once that's under 0.25). It also tops the timer up to only 75% of the time before (4, 3, 2.25 s...), so one trick spammed on the flat can't keep a combo alive. The multiplier caps at ×10.
- **HUD**, bottom center under the skier. Each trick pops up as a line: name, points, and Clean / Sketchy / Switch landing / Repeat. The last three stack, the older ones dimmer; in a window under 400 px tall (a small embed) only the newest shows. While you slide a rail or box, or press a butter, a line under them names it as it goes (a slide counts its meters, a butter its turn). Below them, a small enamel trail-sign panel shows the combo's points, an orange multiplier tag, and the timer as a draining orange bar ("Carving" shows while flow is slowing it). With under a second left on the snow the bar turns red and pulses (not with reduced motion). When a combo ends, the panel shows for a few seconds what it banked ("Banked", or an orange "New best combo!"), or the struck-out total and "Combo lost". "Best combo" for this map sits bottom left, above the frame-rate readout. Everything is sized by the window's height, so it fits at 1366×768 and in a small embed.
- **Best combo** per map, saved locally (`parkLaps.bests.v1`; without storage it lasts the visit). There is no whole-run total.
- **Cheats:** combos still score with cheats on, but a combo ridden while any cheat was on (or the tuning panel had changed a number) never sets a best; its panel says "cheats on, best not saved".
- **Settings:** a Combo score switch, on by default and saved. Off, nothing is scored or shown.
- **Maps:** the Park, Jam Park, Freeride, Big air and the Test slope. The Slalom has its race timer instead (`"scoring": false` in its map file).
- **Headless check:** `node tools/sim/score.ts` rides a scripted trick off every jump on a Park line, then a kicker onto a rail (straight, then turned sideways), then the whole of the map's longest rail, then butters (a tail butter 180, a nose butter 360, a tail press, an ollie 180), and prints every scoring event and the combo totals.

## Maps (v1: four maps plus the Test slope, all unlocked from the start, and Jam Park on trial beside the Park; Brundage Mountain after v1)
Every map has a start gate themed to its kind of skiing, checkpoint flags, a finish zone, and enough decoration to feel like a real place. Decoration stays inside the performance budget: instanced and merged meshes, and the Low preset thins it out. No real brand names or logos anywhere. Every map is picked from the map-select menu.

1. **Park:** a big, dense terrain park, about 2 to 3 minutes top to bottom, with several lines side by side so you can pick a route, features packed close together, and flatter stretches between sections so speed stays under control. Steep enough everywhere that speed comes easily.
   - Jumps in every size, from small tables to a pro line of huge jumps. The biggest jumps clear a clean 720 (see Jump sizing).
   - Every jump has a rounded knuckle, and landing on it is rideable rather than an instant crash. Include step-ups, step-downs, and hips.
   - Rails and boxes of every kind: flat, down, flat-down, down-flat-down, kinked, and rainbow. Plenty are ride-on at snow level, and plenty are reached off a kicker (jumps onto rails and onto boxes).
   - A rail that runs the whole way from the top of the map to the bottom.
   - At least two halfpipes, a smaller one and a big one, each with a drop-in deck at the top.
   - Decoration: orange safety fencing along the lines, plain banners, a chairlift in the distance, light poles, a groomer parked to the side.
   - Start gate: a wooden start deck under a banner arch.
   - Flags: one at the start of each section.
2. **Jam Park (on trial):** a reworked Park, denser, with bigger features that come together into combos, and lines that cross over. It's a map of its own (`tools/maps/jampark.ts` writes `src/maps/jampark.json`, its own card in map select, after the Park), so the Park stays as it was until play-testing decides which one stays. Inspired by the dense park maps of other freeski games, but its own layout.
   - The Park's frame: lines side by side down four sections (12°, 14°, 19° for the pro zone, 11°) with 4° flats between them, a flag at the top of each section in every line, a wooden start deck, a finish flat. The long rail runs top to bottom along the left edge (↓ brakes on it, as on any rail).
   - Lines, left to right: jib (rails and boxes side by side in pairs, ride-on and off small kickers: flat, down, kinked, jogged, C-, S- and rainbow rails, tubes, bonks, wallrides), combo (rails into jumps into rails, bonks, wallrides), jump (tables in a rhythm, then gap jumps, step-ups and step-downs, one over a parked groomer), transitions (hips, quarterpipes, the small and the big halfpipe) and pro (big tables, then two huge jumps).
   - Small stuff stands alone too (bonk rows, pairs of ride-on jibs), but the lines are built as combos: each line is a list of features laid end to end with a short run between, so one landing sets up the next.
   - Lines cross over: the fences between lines are short runs with openings, low enough to jump (and only as solid as they look); the S1 quarterpipes throw riders across toward the jump line; at the bottom of the pro zone two transfer rails swap the jib and combo lines (a tall tube gaps over a low rail where they cross); in S4 the combo and jump lines meet in a jib garden.
   - Sized for the speed riders carry there (see Jump sizing): the pro zone's big tables have 14° lips and take 17 to 30 m/s; its open gaps clear from about 20 m/s rolled off; the two huge jumps sit in lanes that climb (a 12° rise) before a short steep in-run, so tucked or standing from their flag, riders reach the lip within ~6 m/s of each other (2.6 to 4.9 s of air; a 720 needs 2.74 s).
   - New feature kinds, for any map: quarterpipes and wallrides (`quarter`: one wall of transition, snow or wood, with a deck and a back bank, tapering in at both ends), tubes (`style: 'tube'` on a rail: a fat painted pipe on saddles) and bonks (box styles `table`, `barrels` and `spool`).
   - Decoration: the Park's, plus 38 floodlight towers on the fence runs (never in an opening) and the groomer in the gap.
3. **Freeride:** a grand mountain adventure. You start on the summit and can ride down in any direction, not along one line: about 2 to 3 minutes from the peak to the bottom whichever way you go, with far more variety than the Park.
   - Every face has its own character, for example steep chutes and cliff bands with drops of several heights; open bowls and pillow fields; tree runs; rolling terrain with natural side hits. Ridges between the faces carry wind lips and cornices, and a gully makes a natural halfpipe.
   - Natural features to ride: fallen trees as rails (slides that look like logs), rock and stump jibs, and booters (snow kickers with landings) dug into ridges, gully walls and banks. Some built jumps and rails too, but mostly natural ones.
   - A ski town at the base of one face, fully rideable: chalet roofs as drops and kickers, handrails down stairs, ledges and benches as rails and boxes, a main street, lamp posts and banners. Building walls are solid.
   - Runs end in the town or at a run-out at the base of each other face; each has a finish zone with Back to top, which returns to the summit (no lift ride: chairlifts climb the faces as scenery).
   - Scenery: rock outcrops and boulders, stands of trees (solid in the tree runs) thinning to a treeline, dead snags, wind-scoured ridges, trail signs at forks, and distant peaks around the map. Detailed enough to look good on a fast computer in clear weather (milestone 6f), while Low thins it out.
   - Start gate: a ridgetop start on the summit with a line of flags and a small start hut, like a freeride contest.
   - Flags: on ledges and shoulders across the mountain, at the top of each face's main lines, so a respawn puts you back on the line you were riding.
   - Built by a script like the Park (`tools/maps/`): the mountain's overall shape around the peak, terrain features placed on it (ridges, bowls, gullies, cliffs, chutes, pillows), then fine rocky detail.
4. **Slalom:** two timed courses side by side on a groomed run: a slalom with tight gates and a giant slalom with wider, faster turns. Each course has its own race start house and timing wand, its own gates, run timer, missed-gate count and finish line, and respawn returns you to that course's start house. This map alone has timed runs.
   - Slalom gates bend when you clip them. Giant slalom gates have a plain panel between their two poles.
   - Start gates: a race start house with a timing wand at the top of each course.
   - Decoration: blue course lines dyed in the snow, safety netting along both sides, a finish banner for each course.
5. **Big air:** several big jumps of different sizes and takeoff steepness, from long and mellow to steep and poppy, side by side at the bottom of the hill. Each has its own lane from a shared start. At least two of them clear a 720 (see Jump sizing).
   - Each jump looks and rides differently: at least one is massive all round (in-run, lip, kicker width, table and landing), one has a really steep lip, one is a freestyle aerials kicker (nearly vertical, for flips), and one is a ski jump (a steep in-run, a takeoff table tipped down to its edge, and a long landing hill with distance markers).
   - A short run-in: lanes start soon after the drop-in, with short, steep in-runs.
   - Tables are wider than their kickers, with room to ride round the kicker and huck the knuckle.
   - Start gate: a scaffold drop-in tower, like a big air contest.
   - Decoration: spectator fencing beside the landings, flags, floodlights.
   - Flags: one at the top of each lane.
6. **Brundage Mountain (milestone 9, after v1):** a recreation of the real ski area near McCall, Idaho, built from public data: the terrain from USGS elevation data, the runs and lifts from OpenStreetMap, checked against the owner's own GPS tracks. It should feel like riding the real mountain: the real runs where they really are, under their real names.
   - Start gate: a ridgetop start on the summit. Finish: the base lodge area, with an arch over the main run-out.
   - Chairlifts along the real lift lines, moving like Freeride's (scenery; riding them stays a later idea).
   - Groomed runs cut into the terrain, forest between them, trees spaced out in the gladed areas, rocks where it's steep.
   - Trail signs with the real run names at forks; checkpoint flags at the main junctions (lift tops and major splits).
   - Its own art throughout: no Brundage logo, no copy of their trail map art, nothing suggesting the resort made or endorses the game. Credit for the data ("© OpenStreetMap contributors", USGS) and a "not affiliated" line in the game and the README.
7. **Test slope:** the development map, kept as a playable map: every feature side by side in four lanes (halfpipe, jumps, rails and boxes, slalom) under all four start gates, for trying things out.

### Jump sizing
- Size the 720 jumps from the config: measure the airtime a clean 720 takes at the current spin rate, including winding up and slowing down, then give those jumps at least 20% more airtime than that at the speed the in-run naturally reaches.
- Every landing is long enough that riding in at full speed with a full pop still lands on the slope, not past it onto flat.
- In a dense line, the snow after a landing is long enough that popping at the top speed riders carry there lands before the next takeoff, not on it. Open gaps (gap jumps, step-ups, step-downs) have nothing to land on short: size them to clear at the slowest speed riders roll off at there.
- Casing a knuckle at low speed is a rough landing, not a crash.

### Mechanics the maps need
- Done in 5a: the halfpipe (wall riding follows the surface normal; airs launch straight up and land back in the transition), ride-on rails and boxes at snow level, slalom gates with flex, the missed-gate check and the run timer.
- Done in 5b: terrain with varied pitch (the fall line as pitch sections, plus lanes: bands with their own fall line that start and end level with the slope beside them), rails that follow the snow, and the map-select menu.
- Done in 5c-1: terrain built around a peak that can be ridden down in any direction (spokes for each face and ridge, then ridges, gullies, cliff bands, wind lips and cornices, bowls, pillows and pillow lines, bumps, run-out dips and flat pads, with a rim of hills past the edge), a play area shaped as a polygon, several finish zones on one map, named runs, decoration placed by height and steepness, and the trail map. The snow is built in chunks around the camera, coarser in the distance, and decoration is culled in 2D tiles, with simple stand-in trees far away. Each quality preset sees its own distance: Low 240 m, Medium and Auto 450 m, High 800 m.
- Done in 5c-2: buildings and terraces as map features (roofs you ride, drifts up to them and landing drifts below, solid walls, stairs and handrails), rail and box looks (fallen logs, handrails, ledges, benches, rocks, stumps), booters with no dyed lip, finish arches on wide run-outs, trail signs, crags, moving chairlifts, swaying cloth, chimney smoke, town props, and a backdrop of sky and distant ranges past the far plane.
- Done in 5e: takeoffs of any steepness (a takeoff face is never a wall, and popping off one steeper than a wall pushes straight up), and lanes that blend bank to bank.
- Done for Jam Park (after 6f): quarterpipes and wallrides (wall airs launch straight up like the halfpipe's, and over snow that isn't a wall the body levels out to it, `air.wallLevel`), tube and bonk looks for rails and boxes, fences only as solid as they look, and ↓ braking on rails.
- Done in 5d: several timed courses on one map (each its own start house and wand, gates, clock, missed-gate count and finish), giant slalom gates with a panel each side, and hillsides folded into spurs and hollows on a slope map.

Each map defines: name, heightfield (generated from parameters or loaded from a small grayscale PNG), features, spawn, start gate type, flags, finish zone, decoration sets, and sky and fog colors. Large maps split their terrain into chunks with frustum culling, so only what's inside the fog distance gets drawn.

## Look
- Low-poly and flat-shaded, close to Shredsauce's style but entirely original.
- Snow uses white and blue-gray vertex-colored faces. Features use wood and metal colors. Trees are instanced low-poly cones.
- The skier is a low-poly segmented body (helmet, torso, arms, legs) on a simple skeleton. Poses are procedural: stance, crouch, tuck, the 10 grabs and 4 style moves, nose and tail presses, and the bail tumble. Skis and poles are separate meshes.
- **Outfit:** colors, headwear and skis, chosen in the Locker and saved locally (below).
- Sky is a gradient, with matching fog per map and weather.

## Locker: outfit, headwear and skis
- Opened from the title and pause menus. A live preview of the skier turns slowly beside the choices. It reuses the one WebGLRenderer (a scissored viewport, or the paused world when opened from pause), never a second WebGL context.
- **Colors** for the helmet, goggles (frame, lens and strap; the lens is a tinted mirror, lighter at the top), jacket, pants, boots and mittens, each picked from one shared palette of about 16 to 20 swatches that look good together. Arrow keys move across the swatches.
- **Headwear:** a helmet; a helmet with a Santa hat over it (red, a white band, and a pom-pom that sways like the banners' cloth); or a jack-o'-lantern: a full carved pumpkin head with a carved face instead of goggles, whose eyes and mouth glow at night. The Santa hat and the pumpkin keep their own colors.
- **Skis:** the ski type and one of its 3 to 4 graphics (see Skis), with stat bars.
- The defaults are today's look: the current outfit colors (the palette includes them), the helmet, and the park ski with lime tips as its first graphic.
- Everything is saved locally; the defaults work without storage. The rig rebuilds its geometry only when the outfit changes, never per frame.

## Skis
Three ski types, each with its own model, 3 to 4 top sheet graphics to pick from, and its own stats. Stats are multipliers on the base numbers in `config.ts` (a `skis` block there). The park ski's multipliers are all 1, so it rides exactly like today's skiing and every map stays tuned for it. The physics treats all three skis as the same length, so rails and landings don't shift between them.

| Ski | Model | Better at | Worse at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Park | Today's twin tip: both ends turned up | Spins (the baseline) | Nothing; it's the baseline |
| Big mountain | Longer looking and much wider, a long early-rise nose, a small tail rise | Bigger drops (higher `impactMax`), landing with the nose further up or down (higher `pitchMax`) | Slower spins and flips, but still able to clear a 720 on the 720 jumps (they have 20% spare airtime) |
| Carving | A narrow waist with a clear sidecut, a flat race tail, a low tip | Holds an edge at speed (`grip`), keeps speed through turns (`carveFriction`), sharper turns when fast (`turnRateFast`), higher top speed (less friction and drag) | Stiff landings (lower `pitchMax` and `impactMax`), slower spins, twitchy riding switch (catches an edge more easily going backwards) |

- The Locker shows stat bars for each ski: Spins, Landings, Top speed, Grip. They come from the multipliers.
- **Graphics:** original art painted as vertex colors on a grid of small faces across the top sheet, so it shows on Low without textures. Park graphics are bold and graphic (stripes, blocks, checks). Big mountain graphics are colorful hand-drawn art in the spirit of artist-series skis (painted mountains, sunsets, swirls, critters). Some graphics also paint the base, and some are pairs: one picture across both skis, so the two go together (all the big mountain graphics are). Carving graphics are race style (speed stripes, gradients). No real brands, logos, text or copied artwork.
- Poles are the same for every ski.

## Cheats
- A Cheats screen, reached from the title and pause menus, with toggles that combine freely:
  - **Low gravity:** gravity scaled down, for long floaty airs.
  - **Super fast:** a much higher top speed (less friction and drag), and quicker, stronger pole pushes that keep helping up to a higher speed. Everything else runs at normal speed.
  - **Moon jump:** pops launch far higher.
  - **Slow motion:** the whole game runs slower (the time fed to the fixed-step loop is scaled), so tricks are easier to time.
  - **No crashes:** every landing rides away, however bad: the skier is set upright on the snow instead of bailing. Hitting an obstacle stops you without a crash.
  - **Turbo spins:** spins, flips and Lincoln loops rotate much faster.
- Every cheat's numbers live in `config.ts` (a `cheats` block) and layer on top of the base numbers and the ski's multipliers, so turning a cheat off restores exactly what was there.
- **Tuning panel:** the Cheats screen has a button that opens the live tuning panel (lil-gui) in any build. It loads only when opened (its own chunk), so the first load is unchanged. Its changes last until reload, and it has a reset button. Typing into it never steers the skier.
- Cheats start off on every visit (never saved). While any cheat is on, or the tuning panel has changed a value, a small "Cheats on" tag shows in a corner.

## Weather
- **Bluebird:** a clear blue-sky day: deep blue overhead down to a light blue haze at the horizon (the user asked for a clear sky in 6f), with the far scenery on Medium and High.
- **Overcast:** flat gray light, low contrast, closer haze.
- **Blizzard:** moody but playable, with about 100 to 120 m of view on every preset. Falling snow blows sideways in gusts under a gray-white sky, and more snow blows off the ridges.
- **Night:** a dark blue sky with a moon and stars, blue moonlight on the snow, and a headlamp glow ahead of the skier. Floodlights switch on at the Park, Big air and Slalom with pools of light on their runs, the windows and street lamps glow in Freeride's town, and the jack-o'-lantern's face glows.
- Picked on each map card before riding, or from the pause menu mid-run (it changes live). The last pick is remembered for every map. Default: bluebird.
- Lighting stays within CLAUDE.md's rule (one directional plus one hemisphere light). Weather changes their colors and strength, the sky and the fog. The headlamp and floodlight pools are faked (for example, additive light decals on the snow), not real lights.
- Each map's sky and fog colors are its bluebird look. The other weathers derive from them unless a map overrides them.
- Low keeps its fog within its 240 m budget; the blizzard brings it closer.

## Snow effects
- One pooled effects system with a fixed budget and no per-frame allocations. The render layer reads sim state and events. Low gets fewer particles and shorter tracks.
- Spray when carving hard (scaled by edge angle and speed), and a sheet of spray when braking or hockey stopping.
- Ski tracks: a ribbon behind each ski that fades out, broken in the air and on rails, the same everywhere (powder tracks were wider and darker at first; the user chose one look).
- A puff of snow on hard landings and a burst on a crash.
- Sparks off metal rails (none off boxes, logs or other wood).
- Powder clouds: bigger, fluffier spray off-piste on Freeride. Groomed snow is the Park, Slalom, Big air, the Test slope, and Freeride's named runs and town. The rest of Freeride is powder.
- Wind blowing snow off ridges, wind lips and cornices, more in a blizzard. The blizzard's falling snow uses the same system.

## Camera
- Third-person chase from behind and above, smoothed. It pulls back as speed rises and swings around when you ride switch.
- It doesn't clip into terrain.
- In the air it keeps the horizon stable and does not spin with the skier.
- Click-and-drag with the mouse (or a finger) orbits the camera around the skier. Standing still, the view stays where you leave it; once riding, it eases back behind the skier a moment after you let go.

## Audio (sound effects only)
- Built with Web Audio, all procedurally (the user's pick in milestone 7): filtered noise, oscillators and envelopes, made at startup. No sound files, nothing to credit, nothing to download.
- Nothing is made or heard before the first key press or click; the sound pauses while the tab is hidden.
- Snow carve hiss scaled by speed and edge angle (softer and duller in powder), a gritty scrape when braking or hockey stopping, and rushing wind by speed: quieter on the snow, fuller and brighter in the air.
- Pop, soft and hard landing thuds by impact, pole plants, rail scrape (metal rails and handrails ring; boxes, logs, tubes and bonk jibs rumble) with a clank or knock getting on, and the bail tumble.
- Menus: a soft wooden click when a plank or button is picked, and a much quieter tick when keyboard focus moves.
- Combos (when scoring is on): a soft chime when a combo banks, a brighter one for a new best, a muffled note when one is lost. Tricks themselves are silent.
- Weather ambience: howling wind that follows the snow's gusts in a blizzard, a quiet hush at night, light wind on overcast, nearly nothing on bluebird.
- A crowd wherever spectators stand (Big air's fences and landings, a few at the Slalom and in Freeride), murmuring louder the more stand near, cheering a clean landing within 70 m of them (not a sketchy one) and jeering a crash (a sinking "ooooh", boos, a laugh).
- Chairlift hum near the lifts (loudest at the stations) on every map that has them, and chatter in Freeride's town, fading with distance and panned toward where they are.
- The riders on a chairlift (asked for after milestone 7) cheer a clean landing within 30 m of the cable (whoops, a wolf whistle, mitten claps) and heckle a crash (an "ohhh", a boo, a laugh), from up where the chairs are. Only Freeride's lifts, and one of Big air's along the edge, can be ridden under; the Park's, Jam Park's and the Slalom's run outside the boundary.
- The ambience sits well under the riding sounds (a quiet bed, about a quarter as loud as the carve; the blizzard about half).
- Slow motion (a cheat) lowers every game sound's pitch with the game's speed. Pausing (any menu, the trail map) silences the game's sounds; menu clicks still play.
- Master and effects volume sliders plus a mute toggle in Settings, heard live while dragging and saved.

**Known issues**
- Not tested on a real school Chromebook. With the CPU slowed 4×, Low takes ~1.6 to 3 ms of CPU per frame (42 to ~115 draw calls); a jump to a new spot spikes one frame to 30 to 50 ms. GPU cost unmeasured.
- Auto is the same as Medium until milestone 8's frame-rate scaling. `?quality=` still overrides the saved preset for a visit.
- With the CPU slowed 4×, the first time Settings or Controls opens costs one ~60 / ~30 ms frame (layout), while the game is already paused; later opens and pausing or resuming cost 6 to 18 ms. Riding on Low is unchanged (~1.6 ms per frame).
- Key names follow key positions on a US keyboard (bindings use `KeyboardEvent.code`).
- Sound: every level, pitch and envelope was set by measuring rendered WAVs, not by ear; it needs a listen and tuning. The crowd and town are suggestive murmurs (formant-filtered buzz), not real voices. Places are panned by the camera's direction but not muffled behind terrain. Audio runs on its own thread, so its cost on a real Chromebook's audio thread (about ten looping voices and a dozen filters) is unmeasured. The first click's own menu click is silent (the sounds are still being made).
- Cheats: Low gravity slows in-runs too (gravity drives them), so its airs are slower as well as longer; with Low gravity or Moon jump, flights that fall steeply onto flat snow can land nose-down and crash (No crashes covers it). Moon jump in a halfpipe is still capped by `air.wallLaunchMax`. The tuning panel can cover the right of a menu in a small embed (close it from its own button).
- Far forests show as flat dark facets past the far plane (Freeride; the Slalom on Medium); far stand-in trees are plain cones.
- Park: huge jumps sized for tucking from their flag.
- Freeride: couloirs reach ~36 m/s; the camera can't see past a convex rollover; trail map labels crowd.
- Slalom: course times come from a bot that steers more finely than keys.
- Big air: no speed-check rise below the tower, so landings are sized for tucking from it; full-speed knuckle hucks overshoot Massive, Steep and Aerials; a 720 off the aerials kicker corks into a flip; the outer lanes are a ~160 m swing out; the tower's deck needs a pole push.
- Skier model: thighs raised high (crouch, tuck, grabs) still cross the jacket between and under the legs, where a single-sheet jacket can't open round them; the longer jacket shows it a little more (clearance.ts reports it). In a tuck the poles press into the baggy sleeve under the arm (by design; ~12 cm by clearance.ts), and the inside pole touches the thigh in a hard tucked carve. A pole push from a tuck still adds its forward bend on top of the tuck.
- Grabs and style moves: they don't change the physics, so landing while holding a spread eagle or daffy rides away like any landing. The japan stands the ski up behind the back, so from the chase camera it covers part of the body.
- Butters: a nollie or ollie has no pitch kick (the skis don't tip off the pressed end), the Turbo spins cheat doesn't speed up butter pivots, and the drawn skis tip about the pressed end without the sim's contact moving there.
- Skis: the carving ski's top speed outruns the Park's landings: a rider who tucks straight down every jump flies 12 to 50 m past them onto flat and crashes (checking speed before each lip, it lands them all, like the park ski). Tucked from the top of Big air's tower it crashes the big kickers; from the flag it lands them, except Steep. The big mountain graphics are pixel art at their grid, and in the stance the two skis stand apart, so a pair picture has a gap down its middle. The Locker's preview shows only the top sheets (the bases show in the rack, and in game in the air). Merged top-sheet faces leave T-junctions along each row's edge (no gaps seen). Graphics only appear on the top sheet; the base is one color.
- Weather: GPU cost unmeasured on real hardware (night adds a per-vertex pool loop and a little per pixel). Flakes are flat unlit diamonds; the riding camera looks well down the slope, so mostly the horizon's haze shows of the sky. Night pools light the snow and features, not the skier or chairlifts. The moon hangs in one direction on every map (ahead-left of the Park's fall line).
- Snow effects: particles are low-poly chunks that shrink away rather than fade (no per-particle transparency), so powder clouds read as clumps of snow rather than mist. Falling snow from `setWeather` shares the particle pool, so heavy snowfall on Low crowds out the spray (6f should give it a budget of its own, and size the flakes: at 3.5 to 5 cm they're hard to see). Particles don't collide with the snow; they fade before they sink far. Track points sit on the drawn snow at their ends, so a track can dip into a facet's crease between them (the polygon offset hides it so far). The track's head follows the skis' heading, not the feet, so it sits a few centimeters off in a hard angulated turn.
- Locker: opened from pause, the circling camera can pass through a nearby wall or start house. In a small embed the Locker scrolls, and its preview gets short. The default helmet, goggle frame and jacket are the palette's Coal (#2c3037), a hair off the old #2a2d32 and #30343a.

Rotation names come from the body's axes, so a spin with its axis tipped near 45° can read as a cork or a rodeo depending on which turned more. No halfpipe-specific names (alley-oops); a straight wall air is "Air", so back-to-back pipe airs need different tricks to keep scoring. Rail-to-rail transfers aren't named. - Test slope: invisible walls at the berm tops; riding off a rail sideways crashes.
